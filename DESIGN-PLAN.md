# Artist website design plan

## Direction

Design the website as a contemporary exhibition catalogue: generous artwork, precise typography, and a deliberately edited sequence. Let the work supply the color, texture, and visual tension.

The existing site has restraint, but its presentation needs more deliberate image selection, scale, and hierarchy. These changes will have a greater impact than changing the font or adding whitespace alone.

This plan follows a review of all six pages’ structure and content, the local image collection, and selected rendered desktop and mobile views. It is a proposal; implementation has not begun.

## Principles

- Preserve complete compositions and their original proportions.
- Introduce the artwork directly, then provide installation context.
- Distinguish complete works, details, and installation photographs.
- Use a consistent grid while allowing image scale to respond to the artwork.
- Make typography and navigation quiet, legible, and predictable.
- Use whitespace to group related content and establish rhythm.
- Prioritize ordinary scrolling, with enlargement available for closer inspection.

The relationships between graphite, vivid digital color, gold leaf, and irregular paper edges should guide the presentation. Monochrome and colored works shown together make their transformation legible. Installation photographs establish physical scale and exhibition context, but should generally follow direct views of the work.

## Homepage

### Structure

1. Quiet header: a small `gautama` wordmark at left, with `Work`, `About`, and `Contact` at right.
2. A substantial, uncropped Bodhisattva presentation, ideally pairing monochrome and colored works, with a discreet series title and link.
3. Three further series sections, each with one carefully chosen image and a consistent caption.
4. A small footer containing email and Instagram links.

### Layout

On desktop, use a narrow text column and a broad image column. Allow image widths to vary within that shared grid: portrait drawings can sit narrower, while diptychs and triptychs expand. Maintain common alignment to keep these variations coherent.

On mobile, stack each image above its caption and preserve its natural proportions.

Replace the current three-column square grid. Four series leave an isolated fourth tile, the horizontal gutters feel tight, and square crops cut into multi-panel compositions. Asiatic especially needs its full horizontal span.

### Proposed sequence

1. Bodhisattva
2. Contextual Modernism
3. Asiatic
4. American Vedas

Placing Contextual Modernism next to Bodhisattva emphasizes their shared monochrome/color relationships.

## Series pages

### Shared structure

1. Series title and a brief introduction.
2. Large complete artwork views with captions.
3. Selected details adjacent to their corresponding works.
4. An installation photograph.
5. The fuller statement and a clear link to the next series.

Use normal scrolling as the main browsing experience, with click-to-enlarge for closer inspection. Replace the long carousel as the primary presentation. The current Bodhisattva carousel contains 22 slides without clearly distinguishing individual works from details.

Captions should identify title, year, medium, and dimensions wherever those facts are available. Label close-ups as details. Do not invent missing artwork metadata.

### Bodhisattva

- Lead with complete drawings and emphasize the relationship between monochrome and colored works.
- Follow each work with one or two revealing details of gold leaf, marks, or paper.
- Finish with installation context.
- Move the current opening installation photograph later in the sequence: much of its frame is ceiling, wall, and floor, leaving the drawings relatively small.
- Edit repeated close-ups into a deliberate selection.

### Contextual Modernism

- Open with the complete diptych.
- Show the individual compositions next, followed by selected details of their distortions.
- Review the additional local photographs for inclusion; the current page renders only one image.

### Asiatic

- Give the triptych a wide, uninterrupted presentation.
- Select one of the two nearly identical available photographs.
- A straight-on photograph of the complete work would materially improve this page. Treat this as a photography need, rather than trying to solve it through layout alone.

### American Vedas

- Lead with the individual drawings. The red-and-black Shiva image is a strong opening candidate.
- Preserve the white space within each drawing.
- Follow the individual works with a photograph of the three pieces installed together.

## Typography and spacing

- Retain a neutral sans serif and the restrained lowercase wordmark.
- Establish a consistent hierarchy: captions around 14px, body copy around 17–18px, and moderately sized page titles.
- Improve the balance between the currently small series labels and large About-page CV headings.
- Keep related images, captions, and controls visually connected.
- On mobile, use natural image heights instead of the carousel’s tall fixed image area, which creates excess blank space and separates controls from the photograph.
- Preserve ample space between distinct works and sections without introducing arbitrary gaps.

## About page

- Retain the portrait, which connects the artist to the work.
- Pair it with a shorter biography in the opening section.
- Place a compact CV beneath that section.
- Format exhibition years in a narrow column, with exhibition and venue information aligned alongside.
- Reduce the size of exhibition and education headings.
- Include a visible contact line.

## Writing and content

- Remove repetition from the biography, especially repeated explanations of cultural and religious change.
- Tighten statements that explain the same artistic process multiple times.
- Provide a brief introduction before the work and make the fuller statement available later in the page.
- Correct typographical and grammatical errors, including “chage” in the biography.
- Preserve the artist’s meaning and verify factual details when editing captions and the CV.

## Implementation priorities

1. Replace square homepage crops with a composition-led layout and a clear focal point.
2. Curate the image selection and sequence for each series.
3. Rebuild series pages around complete works, associated details, and installation context.
4. Refine typography, shared alignment, spacing, and mobile image sizing.
5. Restructure the About page and tighten the copy.
6. Add clear navigation between series and visible contact information.

## Review criteria

- Artwork compositions remain intact on desktop and mobile.
- The homepage has a clear focal point and a coherent sequence through all four series.
- Visitors can distinguish complete works from details and installation photographs.
- Every included image contributes information rather than repeating another view unnecessarily.
- Mobile layouts avoid large empty image containers and detached controls.
- Captions and body copy are comfortable to read.
- Navigation makes it easy to return to the work, continue to another series, and contact the artist.
- The About page presents the biography and CV with a balanced, compact hierarchy.
